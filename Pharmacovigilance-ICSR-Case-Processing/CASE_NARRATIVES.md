# Synthetic Pharmacovigilance Case Narratives

**Every case is fictional and for training only.** None is a validated regulatory ICSR.

## PV-2026-005 | Suspected amoxicillin-associated anaphylaxis

A spontaneously reported fictional adult patient developed a sudden anaphylactic reaction following the reported start of amoxicillin. The report came from a pharmacist, was flagged life-threatening, and states that the suspected drug was withdrawn. The report suggests a need for urgent clinical review and requests additional evidence about management, timing, and outcome.

- **Reported seriousness:** Life-threatening (subject to confirmation).
- **Assessment:** Suspected association; causality not established.
- **Follow-up:** Confirm treatment chronology, emergency care, seriousness details, and outcome.

## PV-2026-002 | Severe abdominal pain reported with ibuprofen

A fictional consumer report describes severe abdominal pain after ibuprofen. **Severe** here denotes symptom intensity; the available record does not document a regulatory **serious** criterion.

- **Reported seriousness:** No criterion documented.
- **Assessment:** Do not equate severe intensity with seriousness.
- **Follow-up:** Clarify whether hospitalization, a medically important event or another seriousness criterion occurred.

## PV-2026-007 | Incomplete potential ICSR

A fictional report records suspected metformin use, nausea, and a pseudonymized patient identifier, but the **reporter is not identifiable**. The entry therefore lacks one of the four minimum ICSR data elements and remains a follow-up candidate rather than a validity-confirmed ICSR.

- **Validity:** Incomplete minimum information.
- **Assessment:** No established causality.
- **Follow-up:** Request an identifiable reporter/contact and preserve initial report details.
